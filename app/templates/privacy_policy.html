Privacy Policy

This page explains what data Rulezet collects, why, and how you can control it. Last updated: {{ privacy_last_updated | default('2 November 2026') }}.

Rulezet is an open-source, community-driven platform for sharing cybersecurity detection rules. This policy covers the official instance at rulezet.org. Anyone can also self-host Rulezet from its source code — if you are using a self-hosted instance, this default policy may have been customized by that instance's operator, who is responsible for it instead of the Rulezet project.

1. Public or private — who sees what

The short version: rules are public, almost everything else is yours to choose. Here is who can see what.

2. Information we collect
  • Account data — username, email address and a securely hashed password (or your SSO identity if you sign in that way). An account whose email is not verified within 24 hours is deleted automatically.
  • What you publish — rules, bundles, comments, tags, votes, reports, edit proposals, forks and merge requests.
  • Messages — the messages, images and reactions you send in direct conversations and channels, and which messages you have read (shown to the others as “Seen by”).
  • Preferences — notification and email choices, alerts, labels, theme.
  • API keys — if you create one, to authenticate your scripts. Treat it like a password.
  • Activity logs — actions such as creating or editing a rule, joining a channel or a moderation, for accountability and abuse prevention.
  • Technical data — standard web server logs (IP address, browser user-agent, time of each request).
3. How we use it
  • To run your account and let you publish, share and discuss detection rules.
  • To deliver your messages and notifications — and only the emails you turned on (alert emails, “Messages by email”).
  • To keep an audit trail of changes, and to resolve disputes or abuse.
  • To protect the platform: spam limits, rate limits, blocking abusive accounts.
  • To show anonymous, aggregate statistics (for example the number of rules).

We do not sell your personal data and we do not use it for advertising.

4. Sharing, federation and other services
  • Federation — administrators can sync rules with other Rulezet instances through connectors. Only public content can be replicated; private bundles and messages are never federated.
  • Instance statistics — a self-hosted Rulezet sends a daily ping to rulezet.org with its address, version and number of rules and bundles. No personal data.
  • AI features — when you ask for an AI analysis or use the assistant, the rule and your request go to the AI provider this instance is configured with (a local Ollama model, or a provider such as Anthropic or OpenAI).
  • Link previews — a link to a public rule, bundle or post shared in a chat app or on a social network shows its title and description. Private content never gets a preview.
  • Fonts and libraries — some pages load fonts from Google Fonts and code libraries from public CDNs (cdnjs, jsDelivr); your browser contacts them like for any website.
5. Cookies and local storage

Only what is needed to run the site: a session cookie to keep you signed in (plus the “remember me” cookie if you tick it) and a CSRF token protecting your forms. Your browser also keeps a few preferences on your own device — light / dark theme, message drafts, what the Enter key does. No third-party tracking or advertising cookies.

6. How long we keep it
  • Your account and content: as long as your account exists.
  • Deleted rules go to a trash first and are removed for good after the instance’s retention period, or sooner on request.
  • A deleted message is hidden from everyone; its text stays in the database so that abuse can still be checked by an administrator. “Delete for me” hides a conversation for you only.
  • Accounts whose email was never verified: deleted after 24 hours.
  • Activity logs and server logs: kept longer, for security and audit.
7. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the EU / UK GDPR), you can access, correct, export or delete your personal data. Most of it can be changed from your account settings; for a full export or the deletion of your account, contact us (below).

8. Security

Passwords are stored as salted hashes, never in plain text. Access rights (visitor / signed-in user / owner / administrator) are checked on every request and administrator actions are logged. Messages are limited against spam, uploaded images are re-encoded, and user content is cleaned before it is shown. No online service can promise absolute security: use a strong, unique password.

9. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as the platform evolves. Important changes are announced on the project’s GitHub repository. Using Rulezet after an update means you accept the new version.

10. Contact

Questions about this policy or your data? Reach out via GitHub Discussions or open an issue on the rulezet-core repository.
